# Launch Strategy

## The launch is not the product announcement — it is the momentum machine

A launch works when it creates a state change in the market's awareness before the product is available. By the day of launch, your best customers should already be sold. The launch day post is for them to share, not for them to decide.

## Pre-launch phase (T-minus 2–4 weeks)

- **Seed the problem, not the solution.** Post about the painful status quo your product solves. Build shared language for the problem before revealing what fixes it.
- **Name the enemy.** What is the existing broken thing your product replaces? Spreadsheets, agencies, manual processes, outdated tools? Name it explicitly. People rally around shared enemies.
- **Build a waitlist with a specific promise.** Not "join our waitlist" — "join 2,400 people getting early access at 60% off." The specificity signals real demand already exists.
- **Create the founding story moment.** Who built this, why, what failed before this existed? Founders tell their story once, and it gets shared forever.

## Launch day execution

- **Launch in tiers, not blasts.** Email list first (they opted in). Then social. Then press/paid. Each tier should reference the previous tier's reaction ("1,200 people signed up in the first 6 hours").
- **Make the ask singular.** One CTA per launch touchpoint. Decision paralysis kills launches — "sign up," "share," "read," and "watch" cannot all appear in the same message.
- **Capture social proof in real time.** Have a system ready to screenshot, quote, and reshare the first wave of responses within hours.

## Post-launch momentum (T-plus 1–7 days)

- **Numbers update posts perform.** "We crossed 1,000 customers at 11am" is shareable content. Update daily during launch week.
- **Address the objection that didn't get answered.** Every launch surfaces 1–3 recurring questions. Write a direct response post within 48 hours.
- **Reward early adopters publicly.** Name your first 10 customers (with permission). People want to be next.

## Positioning frameworks

- **Against-the-category launch:** "This is not a [category]. It's a [new category]." Works when the existing category has baggage.
- **Speed launch:** Lead with time-to-value. "Campaign live in 12 minutes." Benchmark against the slow alternative.
- **Proof-first launch:** Lead with a customer result, reveal the product that produced it second.
- **Transparency launch:** Show the real numbers (MRR, churn, retention). Vulnerability in B2B creates enormous trust.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Never launch on a Monday — social engagement is lowest. Tuesday–Thursday are peak B2B window.
- Never treat Product Hunt as the launch itself — it's distribution for a launch that's already happening elsewhere.
- Never end launch week without a "lessons learned" post. The retrospective compounds into future launch content.
- Never lead with feature lists. Lead with the transformation: before state → after state, made specific.
