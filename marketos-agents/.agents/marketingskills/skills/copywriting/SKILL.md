# Copywriting

## The hierarchy of copy quality

1. **True** — Every claim is verifiable. Fake specificity ("millions of users") destroys trust when buyers check.
2. **Specific** — Numbers, names, timelines, and results instead of adjectives.
3. **Surprising** — Violates a low expectation the reader had before they read it.
4. **Earned** — Built to a conclusion through evidence, not stated as an assertion.

Most AI copy fails at #2 and #3, defaulting to safe, forgettable language.

## Structural rules for high-converting copy

### Headlines
- The job of a headline is one thing: earn the next sentence. Not to explain the product. Not to list features.
- Best-performing structures: Specific result ("How [Company] cut costs 40% in 90 days"), Counterintuitive claim ("The email we almost didn't send became our top performer"), Question with a non-obvious answer ("Why do the best-funded startups still lose to scrappy competitors?")
- Hard limit: 10 words or fewer for paid ad headlines. Every word must carry weight.

### Body copy
- **Lead with the reader, not the product.** The first 2 sentences are about their situation. The product enters as the solution, never as the subject.
- **One idea per paragraph.** If a paragraph has two distinct points, it has one too many.
- **Concrete over abstract always.** "Reduces churn" → "Cut churn from 6.2% to 2.8% in 45 days."
- **Sentence variety kills scan fatigue.** Mix short punches (3–5 words) with medium explanatory sentences (12–18 words). Never 3 long sentences in a row.

### CTAs
- State the specific outcome of clicking, not the action itself.
  - BAD: "Click here" / "Learn more" / "Get started"
  - GOOD: "See my campaign dashboard" / "Download the 2024 benchmark report" / "Start your first campaign"
- First-person CTAs ("Start my free trial") outperform second-person ("Start your free trial") by 10–25% on average.

## Voice and tone calibration

- **B2B SaaS:** Confident, direct, zero buzzwords. Write as if you're a respected peer, not a vendor pitching.
- **DTC/consumer:** More emotional latitude. Conversational. Self-aware humor is acceptable if the brand voice supports it.
- **Urgency copy:** Never manufactured. Urgency must be real (actual deadline, actual stock limit). Fake urgency teaches readers to ignore future CTAs.

## Words and phrases that trigger immediate credibility loss

Never use: "synergy," "best-in-class," "world-class," "innovative," "cutting-edge," "robust," "seamlessly," "leverage," "empower," "holistic," "transformative," "disruptive" — these are words that have been drained of meaning by overuse. Replace with specifics.

## The edit checklist

Before finalizing any piece of copy, run these checks:
- [ ] Remove every word that doesn't earn its place (cut 20–30% by default)
- [ ] Replace all adjectives with data points where possible
- [ ] Read aloud — if you stumble on a sentence, a reader will too
- [ ] Check the first sentence: does it make a promise that the rest of the piece keeps?
- [ ] Check the last sentence: does it tell the reader exactly what to do next?
