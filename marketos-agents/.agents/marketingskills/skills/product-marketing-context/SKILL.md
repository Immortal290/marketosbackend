# Product Marketing Context

## What product marketing actually does (vs. what most AI agents think it does)

Product marketing is not copywriting. It is the translation layer between product reality and market language. Its primary output is not words — it is positioning, which then produces words.

## Positioning before writing — always

Positioning answers four questions before any copy is written:
1. **Who is this for, specifically?** Not "marketers" — "B2B SaaS founders with 2–20 person marketing teams who've outgrown HubSpot's automation tier."
2. **What does this replace?** Not "the competition" — "the 11-spreadsheet stack + Zapier + Notion doc system they built themselves."
3. **What is the one thing it does better than anything else?** Not "many features" — one sentence a competitor cannot truthfully say.
4. **Why now?** Market conditions change. What changed in the world that makes this product necessary today and unnecessary 3 years ago?

## The Positioning Canvas (run this before briefing any campaign)

```
Market category:    [What shelf does this live on?]
Target customer:    [Job title + company type + current painful behavior]
Key benefit:        [What they get that they couldn't before]
Against:            [Named alternatives: the thing this replaces]
Differentiator:     [Provable claim no competitor can make]
Proof:              [Specific metric or named customer that validates the claim]
```

## Message hierarchy — apply this to all campaign briefs

- **Primary message:** The one thing a distracted reader should remember after 3 seconds.
- **Secondary messages (3–5):** What a moderately interested reader learns by skimming.
- **Tertiary proof points:** What a buyer doing serious evaluation needs to see.

Never let secondary messages appear before the primary message is established. Never make proof points the headline.

## Common product marketing anti-patterns

- **Feature-led messaging:** "We have AI, automation, and integrations" — says nothing about transformation. Always translate to outcome: "Your team ships campaigns in a day instead of a week."
- **False category creation:** Inventing a new category name without educating the market on why the new category matters. Only do this if you have distribution to support the education.
- **Competitive vanity:** "Better than [competitor]" without specifying the dimension and proof. Vague competitive claims destroy trust.
- **Persona inflation:** Claiming your product is for "all businesses" — even when it genuinely can serve many segments, you must focus messaging on one to actually convert any.

## Seasonal and contextual adaptation

When a campaign has a seasonal hook (summer sale, Q4 push, new year), the product messaging must connect the seasonal context to the product's core value — not just slap a seasonal emoji on it.

BAD: "🌞 Summer Sale — 40% off MarketOS!"
GOOD: "Q3 pipeline reviews are in 6 weeks. This is how your competitors are going to outrun you — and what to do about it now."
