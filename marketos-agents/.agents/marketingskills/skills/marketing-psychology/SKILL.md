# Marketing Psychology

## Core principles to apply

- **Specificity beats superlatives.** "800 units sold in 48 hours" beats "amazing results." "Used by 4,200 founders" beats "trusted by thousands."
- **Loss aversion outperforms gain framing for urgency.** "Don't miss the last 200 units" > "Get 200 units now." People fear losing a chance more than they desire the gain.
- **Anchor before revealing.** Show the value/price reference point before the offer, never after. "Agencies charge $12,000 for this — ours is $79/month" lands harder than "$79/month (agencies charge $12k)."
- **Social proof requires specificity.** "4,217 marketers use this" > "Used by marketers everywhere." Named industries beat generic personas: "Used by SaaS founders" > "Used by businesses."
- **Pattern interrupts increase read-through.** The first sentence must violate an expectation. Start with a counterintuitive fact, a contradiction, or a confession — never with the product name.
- **Commitment escalation.** Small yes before big yes. Lead with the easiest possible action, then escalate. A free checklist download converts better to a trial than a direct trial CTA.
- **Curiosity gaps over benefit lists.** "What we removed from our pricing page that doubled conversions" > "10 pricing page best practices." The gap must be closeable only by clicking.

## Cognitive biases to deploy deliberately

- **Framing effect:** Same data, different frame. "95% uptime" vs "down 438 hours/year" describe identical realities with opposite emotional weight.
- **Scarcity + specificity:** "3 enterprise slots left this quarter" > "Limited availability." Must be true or credibility collapses permanently.
- **Authority signaling without claiming authority:** Cite specific numbers, studies, or named customers rather than saying "we're experts." Let the evidence assert authority.
- **Reciprocity trigger:** Give something genuinely useful before asking. Free templates, audits, or data before the CTA — not as bait, as a real gift.

## Anti-patterns — AI slop tells, never use these

- Never restate the product name as the key message. "Official campaign launch for X" is banned — say what makes X worth launching.
- Never use: "game-changer," "elevate your," "unlock your potential," "engineered for excellence," "take your X to the next level," "ranked #1 solution" — dead SEO-spam phrases with zero reader impact.
- Never write a headline you could swap the brand name out of and have it still make sense — it must be true only for THIS product, THIS audience, THIS moment.
- Never open with "Introducing" or "Announcing" — these are press release words, not reader-focused words.
- Never use rhetorical questions as the headline unless the answer is not "no." "Want to grow your business?" — the answer might be no, I'm fine.

## Good vs bad examples

BAD: "Discover Brand X: The Ultimate Game-Changer for Modern Marketers"
GOOD: "The 4pm energy crash doesn't happen when you use this"

BAD: "Unlock your potential with our powerful platform"
GOOD: "Our customers cut campaign setup from 11 days to 4 hours. Here's exactly how."

BAD: "Limited time offer — act now!"
GOOD: "We're keeping this price until Friday midnight (then it goes back to $299). 847 people have already locked it in."
